# Product

## Register

product

## Users

One English-speaking learner of Italian for now, who practises about ten minutes a day, usually on a phone and often at home in the evening. It may open to other learners later, so nothing should assume a single user forever, but nothing should be built for strangers yet either (no sign-up, no onboarding funnel).

## Product Purpose

Spoken Italian practice with Giulia, a bookseller in Rome who talks like a friend. During the call she recasts mistakes by using the right form in her own reply, never by correcting. After the call Claude writes a report, and the mistakes and new words become review items with due dates that steer the next call. Success is the learner calling again tomorrow, and saying things correctly that they got wrong last week.

## Brand Personality

Warm, grown-up, quietly Roman. It should feel like calling a friend who lives in Rome, not like playing a level. Encouraging without empty praise; celebrations are small and earned. Giulia's character carries the warmth; the interface stays calm around her.

## Anti-references

- Kids' games: cartoon overload, confetti on every tap, mascots everywhere, XP, gems, hearts.
- Generic AI chatbots: chat bubbles as the whole interface, sparkle icons, the word "AI" anywhere.
- Corporate e-learning: grey dashboards, progress bars for everything, certificates, course modules.
- Dark tech: neon on black, dark mode by default, glowing gradients.

## Design Principles

1. **The call is the product.** Every screen serves the next ten minutes with Giulia; the call screen gets the most craft.
2. **A friend, not a teacher.** No red marks during the call. Learning shows up after it, in the report and in what she steers toward next time.
3. **English helps, Italian stays.** Help, translations and explanations are English text. Everything you hear is Italian.
4. **Honest progress.** Every number comes from real speech. No fake XP, no inflated streak rewards, no praise that was not earned.
5. **Built for one, ready for more.** Personal first, with no sign-up, and nothing that would block other learners later.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: 4.5:1 text contrast (3:1 for large text), full keyboard operation, screen reader labels on every control, and live regions for call state. Every animation has a calm alternative under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Italian text is marked `lang="it"` so screen readers pronounce it correctly.
